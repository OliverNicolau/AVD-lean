* `RequestProject/Crosscut.lean` proves the cutting of a closed Jordan curve at two of its points into two arcs (`AVD.IsClosedJordanCurve.exists_arc_decomposition`) and the **crosscut theorem** for a Jordan domain in the half that is provable from the Jordan curve theorem alone (`AVD.exists_crosscut_domains`): a crosscut `κ` of a complementary domain `D`, together with the two arcs `γ₁`, `γ₂` into which its endpoints cut the curve, bounds two *disjoint* domains `Ω₁, Ω₂ ⊆ D ∖ κ` with `frontier Ωᵢ = κ ∪ γᵢ`. That these exhaust `D ∖ κ` is the θ-curve theorem, which classically needs Janiszewski's theorem; this is stated explicitly in the docstring and the README.

* What is still missing for full accessibility is precisely the local connectedness of the complementary domains at a boundary point — equivalently, crosscuts of arbitrarily small diameter near that point. I explored the two natural routes (nested access arcs and circular crosscuts) and both reduce to that same statement; this is recorded in the README.
